Synthesizing the Unseeable

Dataset creation for low-dose X-ray restoration · By the Twice Baked AI Team

Every low-dose X-ray restoration model is only as good as the data it was trained on, and training data for this problem cannot exist in the way most machine learning problems expect. This series documents, chronologically and honestly, every method we tried to solve that problem: what worked, what failed, why, and what we learned along the way. Real numbers, real training data, real failures, no smoothing over.

  • Part 1 of 4
    Why the Image Lies
    The physics of low-dose noise, why paired training data can never exist, and the earliest classical attempts at synthetic degradation, hand-written blur, noise, structured artifacts, and haze.
  • Part 2 of 4
    Teaching a Network to Imagine Noise
    Three generative philosophies tried in sequence: GANs, VAEs, and diffusion models, each with a genuine partial success and a specific, diagnosable failure mode.
  • Part 3 of 4
    The Physics Was There All Along
    Going back to raw sensor data instead of processed images, the locked physics noise law that's now this project's current backbone, explained down to the math.
  • Part 4 of 4
    Full Circle
    Self-supervised learning, classical domain adaptation, and the discovery that three independent methods, tried separately, all hit the exact same wall.
